Three ways to lay out a district

Same test case in all three: the Pharmacist Verification district, its full 20 workflows, in their fixed unlock order. Two cleared, one open, the rest sealed until you reach them.

Groups the fixed sequence into four contiguous stretches — the order never changes, only how it's read. Each hub gets its own lamp and name; clearing one is the checkpoint before the lane forks on to the next. Best at hiding the total count: you see one neighborhood at a time, not all 20 at once.

One continuous road, exactly like the Overworld's own lanes, just longer. Every workflow is a stop on the same path, with a small shrine marking every fifth one. Most literal read of "progress," but at 20 stops it's a real scroll — this preview is life-size, not shrunk.

Drops the map metaphor for a level-select list — a house glyph and a state chip per row. Cheapest to build and scales to any count without redesign, but it's the one that stops feeling like a place.
